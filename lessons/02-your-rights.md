# Lesson 2: Your rights, and how to use them
<!-- facts: Your rights under POPIA; POPIA forms -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Name their main rights under POPIA.
- Ask an organisation to correct or delete their information, using Form 2.
- Object to processing, using Form 1.
- Say what the organisation must do once it gets the request.

## Key ideas

**Your rights**, in plain words (full list and section numbers in `facts.md`):

1. **To know** when your information is collected, and when it has been leaked or stolen.
2. **To see it**: ask whether they hold it, and get a copy (lesson 4).
3. **To fix or delete it** if it's wrong, out of date, excessive, misleading, or they got it
   unlawfully or have no right to keep it any longer.
4. **To object** to it being used, on reasonable grounds, and to direct marketing at any time.
5. **To complain** to the Information Regulator (lesson 6), and to sue for damages.

**Start with the organisation.** Find its **information officer**: the person responsible for
POPIA requests. Their contact details are usually in the company's privacy policy or PAIA manual
on its website. Write to them, in writing, and keep a copy.

**Correction or deletion: Form 2.** Say what is wrong and what it should be, with proof if you
have it. The organisation must, as soon as reasonably practicable, fix or delete it, or show you
credible evidence that it's right. If you still disagree, you can ask them to attach a note saying
you asked for a correction. They must tell you what they did.

**Objection: Form 1.** Use it when they rely on their own interests rather than your consent, and
your situation gives you a reason to stop it.

**Not every request wins.** Some information must be kept by law (a bank must keep records, for
example). Deletion isn't automatic; the organisation can explain why it has to keep something.

## Worked example

Johan's medical aid still lists his old address, so his statements go to a stranger. He fills in
Form 2, says the address is out of date and gives the correct one, and emails it to the medical
aid's information officer. He saves the email. If he hears nothing back, he follows up, then
complains to the Regulator (lesson 6).

## Exercise

Ask the learner: a gym keeps your details for years after you cancel and keeps sending offers.
Which right fits, and which form would you use? Then help them draft the request with placeholders
for every personal detail.
