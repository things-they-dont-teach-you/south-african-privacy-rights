# Lesson 8: Staying safe online and on WhatsApp
<!-- facts: SIM swaps -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Decide what to share, and with whom, before handing over personal details.
- Protect their WhatsApp account with two-step verification.
- Recognise the most common tricks for stealing personal information.

## Key ideas

**Ask "why do you need this?"** POPIA says organisations may only collect what is adequate,
relevant and not excessive for their purpose, should usually collect it from you directly, and
must tell you why they're collecting it. You can ask why they want your ID number, and
what they'll do with it. A shop doesn't need a copy of your ID to sell you a kettle.

**WhatsApp:**
- Turn on **two-step verification** in WhatsApp's settings, under Account. It adds a PIN or
  password, so someone with your SIM still can't easily take over your account.
- Never share the six-digit code WhatsApp sends you. A "friend" asking for it, saying it was sent
  to you by mistake, is trying to steal your account.
- Check privacy settings: who can see your profile photo, last seen and status, and who can add
  you to groups.

**Common tricks:**
- **Phishing:** a message that looks like your bank, SARS or a delivery company, with a link. Go
  to the app or website yourself instead of clicking.
- **"Hi Mum, new number":** someone pretending to be family asks for money. Call the old number.
- **Fake jobs and competitions** that ask for your ID, bank details or a "registration fee".
- **Pretend officials** who already know your details (from a breach) to sound real.

**Social media:** posts with your school, your street, your birthday and your child's name help
fraudsters answer security questions. Share less in public.

**If something goes wrong,** go back to lessons 6 and 7: report it, protect your ID, and
complain.

## Worked example

Thabo gets a WhatsApp from an unknown number with his cousin's profile photo: "Sorry, sent you my
code by mistake, please forward it." He doesn't. He phones his cousin, whose account was taken
over that morning, and turns on two-step verification on his own phone.

## Exercise

Ask the learner to open their own WhatsApp privacy settings (without sharing screenshots) and
say which two settings they would change. Then show two made-up messages and ask which is phishing.
