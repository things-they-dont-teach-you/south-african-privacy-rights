# Lesson 7: Your credit record, ID fraud and SIM swaps
<!-- facts: Credit bureaus; Identity fraud; SIM swaps -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Say what a credit bureau holds about them, and get their free report.
- Dispute a wrong entry, and know the deadlines.
- Protect their ID number with SAFPS protective registration.
- Spot a SIM swap and say what to do in the first hour.

## Key ideas

**Credit bureaus** collect information about your accounts and repayments and sell it to lenders,
landlords and some employers. It's personal information, so POPIA applies, and the National
Credit Act adds its own rules. How credit scores and debt work is for the
[money course](https://github.com/things-they-dont-teach-you/south-african-money).

**Your free report.** You can get one free report a year from each bureau (`facts.md`). Check it
for accounts you never opened: that's often the first sign of identity fraud.

**Disputing a wrong entry.** Dispute it with the bureau, in writing, with proof. The bureau must
hide the item while it investigates and resolve it within the time in `facts.md`. If you disagree
with the result, you can complain to the National Credit Regulator.

**Identity fraud** is when someone uses your ID number to open accounts, take loans or buy a phone
contract. If your ID is lost or stolen, or you find accounts that aren't yours:
1. Report it to SAPS and get a case number.
2. Tell the credit provider the account is fraudulent.
3. Apply for free **SAFPS protective registration**. It flags your ID number so lenders take
   extra care. How to apply is in `facts.md`.

**A SIM swap** is when a criminal gets your number moved to their SIM card, so they receive your
bank OTPs. The warning sign: your phone suddenly has no signal while others around you do.
1. Call your network provider immediately, from another phone, and ask them to block the SIM.
2. Call your bank's fraud line and freeze your banking.
3. If the network doesn't sort it out, escalate to ICASA (`facts.md`).

## Worked example

Kagiso applies for a store account and is turned down. Her free credit report shows a cellphone
contract in Durban she never signed. She reports it to SAPS, tells the network it's fraud,
disputes the entry with the bureau, and applies for SAFPS protective registration.

## Exercise

Ask the learner: it's 9pm and your phone shows "No service" while your friend's phone works fine.
What are the first two calls you make, and why in that order?
