# Lesson 5: When your data leaks
<!-- facts: Security compromises -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Say what an organisation must do when personal information is leaked or stolen.
- Say what a breach notice must tell them.
- List the first protective steps to take after a breach.

## Key ideas

**A "security compromise"** is POPIA's name for a data breach: someone unauthorised has accessed
or taken your information, whether by hacking, a lost laptop, or a staff member selling a list.

**The organisation must tell you and the Regulator** as soon as reasonably possible, in writing
(by email, post, its website or the news). It may only delay telling you if police or the
Regulator say it would hamper a criminal investigation. Details in `facts.md`.

**The notice must help you protect yourself.** It should say what could happen, what they're
doing about it, what you should do, and who took the data, if they know.

**What to do after a breach notice:**
1. Read what was taken. A leaked email address is a smaller risk than a leaked ID number and
   bank details.
2. Change the password for that account, and anywhere you reused it.
3. Expect scams that mention the breach. Fraudsters use leaked details to sound convincing.
   Don't trust a call just because the caller knows your ID number.
4. If your ID number or bank details leaked, consider SAFPS protective registration and check
   your credit report (lesson 7).
5. Keep the notice. You may need it for a complaint or claim.

**If you think there was a breach and nobody told you,** ask the organisation in writing, and
complain to the Regulator if they don't answer (lesson 6). You can also sue for damages
(`facts.md`), for which you would need an attorney.

## Worked example

Nomsa gets an email from her online store: a hacker accessed names, phone numbers and delivery
addresses, but not card details. She changes her password, warns her family about fake delivery
SMSes, and saves the email. A week later she gets an SMS "from the store" asking her to confirm
her card. She knows it's a scam because of the breach notice.

## Exercise

Ask the learner: a breach notice says names, ID numbers and payslips were stolen from an
employer. Which three steps would they take first, and why?
