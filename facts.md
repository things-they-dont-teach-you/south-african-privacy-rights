# Facts and figures

**Applies to: the law and official processes as at 11 November 2026.** Privacy rules don't change
every year the way tax does, but forms, fees, contact routes and the opt-out registry do. Check the
date next to each section.

Every fact below comes from the linked official page or Act, on the date shown. Lessons point here
instead of repeating details, so updating this file updates the course. The laws are the
Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (**POPIA**) and the Promotion of Access to
Information Act 2 of 2000 (**PAIA**).

## Your rights under POPIA

Source: [POPIA, section 5](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act), checked 2026-11-11.

You have the right to:

| Right | Section of POPIA |
| --- | --- |
| Be told when your information is collected, and when it has been accessed by someone unauthorised | 5(a), 18, 22 |
| Find out whether an organisation holds your information, and ask for it | 5(b), 23 |
| Ask for it to be corrected, destroyed or deleted | 5(c), 24 |
| Object, on reasonable grounds, to it being processed | 5(d), 11(3)(a) |
| Object at any time to it being used for direct marketing | 5(e), 11(3)(b) |
| Not get unsolicited electronic direct marketing, unless the law allows it | 5(f), 69 |
| Not be subject, in some cases, to decisions made only by automated profiling | 5(g), 71 |
| Complain to the Information Regulator | 5(h), 74 |
| Sue for damages | 5(i), 99 |

## What counts, and the rules for collecting it

Source: [POPIA, sections 1, 6, 10, 11, 12, 18 and 26](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act), checked 2026-11-11.

- "Personal information" covers a living person (and, where it applies, an existing company or
  other juristic person). The Act's list includes: race, gender, age, health, religion, language;
  education, medical, financial, criminal or employment history; ID number, email, address, phone
  number, location and online identifiers; biometrics; your opinions; private correspondence;
  other people's opinions about you; your name together with other information (section 1).
- An organisation may only process it on one of six grounds: your consent; a contract with you;
  a legal duty; protecting your legitimate interest; a public body's legal duty; or its own or a
  third party's legitimate interest (section 11(1)).
- You can withdraw consent at any time; what was done before that stays lawful (section 11(2)(b)).
  Once you object under section 11(3), the organisation may no longer process the information
  (section 11(4)).
- "Special personal information" (religion, race, union membership, politics, health, sex life,
  biometrics, criminal behaviour) has extra protection (section 26).
- Information collected must be adequate, relevant and not excessive for its purpose (section
  10), should usually be collected from you directly (section 12), and you must be told what is
  collected, by whom, why, and whether giving it is voluntary (section 18).
- The Act doesn't apply to purely personal or household activity (section 6(1)(a)).

## Access requests

Sources: [POPIA, section 23](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act);
[Information Regulator PAIA Guide](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/PAIA-Guide-English_20210905.pdf);
[PAIA forms](https://inforegulator.org.za/paia-forms/);
[Information Regulator PAIA manual (fee schedule)](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/PAIA-Manual-NOV-2023-English-Updated.docx.pdf); all checked 2026-11-11.

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Asking whether they hold your information | Free (POPIA s23(1)(a)) |
| Proof of identity | Required (POPIA s23(1)) |
| What you can ask for | The record or a description of it, including who it has been shared with (s23(1)(b)) |
| Fee for the record | A prescribed fee may apply; they must give a written estimate first, and may ask for a deposit (s23(3)) |
| Request form | [PAIA Form 2](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/InfoRegSA-PAIA-Form02-Reg7.pdf), Request for Access to Record |
| Request fee, private bodies | R140.00 (PAIA regulations fee schedule, as printed in the Regulator's PAIA manual) |
| Copies, private bodies | R2.00 per A4 page (same schedule) |
| Time to answer | 30 days, extendable once by up to 30 more days (PAIA Guide 16.1.5) |
| No answer in time | Counts as a refusal ("deemed refusal") you can challenge (PAIA Guide 19.5) |
| Public bodies | Internal appeal first, within 60 days of the decision (PAIA Guide 22.1, 22.7) |
| Private bodies | No internal appeal; go to the Regulator or a court (PAIA Guide 22.1) |
| Deadline to complain to the Regulator about a PAIA decision | 180 days from the decision (PAIA Guide 23.3) |
| Where to send a PAIA complaint | PAIA Form 5 to PAIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za ([Complaints page](https://inforegulator.org.za/complaints/)) |

Every organisation must have a PAIA manual saying how to request records and who its information
officer is (PAIA Guide). Look for it on the organisation's website.

## Direct marketing rules

Sources: [POPIA, section 69](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act);
[Information Regulator Guidance Note on Direct Marketing (December 2024)](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/GUIDANCE-NOTE-ON-DIRECT-MARKETING-IN-TERMS-OF-THE-PROTECTION-OF-PERSONAL-INFORMATION-ACT-4-OF-2013-POPIA.pdf);
[Regulator media statement, 21 April 2026](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/MEDIA-STATEMENT-REGULATOR-NOTES-PUBLISHED-GAZETTE-OF-THE-CONSUMER-PROTECT-ACT-AMENDMENT-REGULATIONS-2026.pdf); all checked 2026-11-11.

- Direct marketing by any electronic communication (the Act names automatic calling machines,
  fax, SMS and email) is prohibited unless you consented or you are the marketer's customer (s69(1)).
- A marketer may approach someone who hasn't refused before **only once** to ask for consent, in
  the prescribed form (Form 4) (s69(2)).
- The customer exception only covers details collected when you bought a product or service, and
  marketing of the company's own similar products, and only if you were given a free, simple
  chance to refuse when your details were collected and in every message since (s69(3)).
- Every marketing message must say who sent it and give contact details to ask for it to stop (s69(4)).
- The Regulator's view is that telephone calls are electronic communication too, and that
  messaging apps, push notifications and direct messages on social media are covered (Guidance
  Note 7.1). The Guidance Note is not binding law.
- Not having registered an opt-out does not count as consent (media statement, 21 April 2026).
- You can object to direct marketing by any channel, at any time (s11(3)(b)).

## Security compromises (data breaches)

Source: [POPIA, section 22](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act), checked 2026-11-11.

- If there are reasonable grounds to believe your information was accessed or taken by someone
  unauthorised, the organisation must notify the Information Regulator and you, unless it can't
  identify you (s22(1)).
- It must do so as soon as reasonably possible after discovering it. It may delay telling you
  only if a law-enforcement body or the Regulator says notice would hamper a criminal
  investigation (s22(2), (3)).
- The notice must be in writing: by post, email, a prominent notice on its website, the news
  media, or as the Regulator directs (s22(4)).
- It must give you enough to protect yourself: possible consequences, what the organisation is
  doing, what it recommends you do, and who took the information, if known (s22(5)).

## Complaints

Sources: [Complaints page](https://inforegulator.org.za/complaints/);
[Rules of procedure for POPIA complaints](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/20211012-InfoReg-RulesOfProcedure-HandlingPOPIAcomplaints-1.pdf);
[POPIA page](https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/);
[POPIA, sections 77, 99 and 109](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act); all checked 2026-11-11.

- Complaints must be in writing: the online portal, or Form 5 by email. You may also visit the
  office, and the Regulator will help anyone put a complaint in writing (POPIA page).
- The Regulator accepts general POPIA complaints (sharing without permission, poor security,
  collecting without your knowledge, ignoring your rights requests) and direct marketing
  complaints (spam calls, SMS, WhatsApp, email after opting out) (Complaints page).
- A complaint includes: your name, ID number if required, address and contact details; your
  reasons; the organisation's name, address and contact details. Add dates, places and any
  documents (Rules 4.4.1, 4.4.4). Form 5 asks for supporting affidavits or documents.
- The Regulator acknowledges a complaint with a reference number within 14 days (Rule 4.3.5).
- It may decide to take no action if, for example, too much time has passed, the complaint is
  trivial, or you didn't use an industry code's complaints process first (POPIA s77). It must
  tell you why.
- Administrative fines for breaking POPIA can go up to R10 million (s109). You can also sue for
  damages, whether or not the organisation meant to cause harm (s99).

## SIM swaps

Source: [ICASA: SIM-card fraud and illegal porting](https://www.icasa.org.za/news/2022/awareness-on-sim-card-fraud-and-illegal-porting-of-numbers-during-the-festive-season), checked 2026-11-11.

- Warning sign: your phone loses signal unexpectedly. Contact your network provider immediately.
- Complain to your network provider first. If it isn't resolved, send full details to
  consumer@icasa.org.za.
- Never share login details, passwords or one-time PINs; don't open links in unsolicited messages.
