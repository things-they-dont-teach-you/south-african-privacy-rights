# Your privacy rights in South Africa

What companies and government may and may not do with your personal information, taught by your
AI assistant: your rights under POPIA, stopping spam calls and SMSes, asking a company what it
holds about you, data breaches, complaining to the Information Regulator, and protecting yourself
from ID fraud and SIM swaps.

## Known gaps

Things we couldn't confirm from an official source on 2026-11-11. Lessons avoid relying on them.

- **Opt-out registry dates.** The NCC says consumers can block direct marketers "from May 2027",
  and its portal already offers consumer registration. It is not clear whether blocks registered
  now are enforceable before May 2027.
- **Fees for your own information.** POPIA says asking whether a company holds your information
  is free, and that a "prescribed fee, if any" may apply to a copy. The PAIA fee schedule sets a
  R140 request fee for private bodies "payable by every requester". We found no official
  statement on whether that fee applies when you ask only for your own personal information.
- **Public body fees.** Request and access fees for government bodies are not listed.
- **Complaint time limits under POPIA.** PAIA complaints have a 180-day limit; we found no fixed
  limit for POPIA complaints, only that the Regulator may decline one if too much time has passed.
- **SAFPS registration length.** The SAFPS site doesn't say how long protective registration lasts.
- **The Credit Ombud.** Its role alongside the National Credit Regulator for credit disputes isn't
  covered; only the NCR route is.
- **WhatsApp menus.** Exact menu paths change between versions, so lesson 8 describes them
  loosely and links WhatsApp's own help.
